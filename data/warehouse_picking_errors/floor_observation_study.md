# Floor Observation Study — Picking Accuracy, RDC-7

> **Synthetic document.** Fictional study, fictional people, fictional numbers. No real Walmart data.
> Owner: industrial-engineering. Author: Priya Nandakumar. Fieldwork 2026-07-13 to 2026-07-17. Last updated: 2026-07-24.

## Method

24 hours of direct observation, spread across all three shifts. 1,180 picks were watched at the face. 31 picking errors and 44 self-corrected near-misses were recorded.

The observed error rate of 26.3 per 1,000 picks is far above the recorded mispick rate of 3.9 per 1,000 lines. That is expected. Most observed errors were caught downstream at pack-out or by the picker, and only an error that reaches a store and is claimed within 72 hours enters the mispick log. The recorded figure is a floor, not a total.

## What was observed at the face

24 of the 31 observed errors occurred in aisles 31 to 44. 19 of those 24 were a reach to the immediately adjacent pick face rather than the one on the pick list.

Median hesitation time at a face belonging to a confusable pair was 4.1 seconds, against 1.3 seconds at other faces.

Experienced associates on all three shifts pause and scan the case barcode before placing it in the tote. The scan-verify step is optional in Corvus WMS 7.2 for ambient full-case picks and is not enforced.

Scanning added 2.8 seconds per line on average. The 2.8-second figure is an average over 38 observed scan-verify picks and should not be treated as a validated time standard.

## Desk review of the slot map

A desk review of the post-reslot slot map identified 41 pairs of items whose descriptions are identical in the first 18 characters, which is all the RF terminal displays.

38 of the 41 pairs are slotted face-adjacent, within 1.2 metres of one another. 3 are not. The median separation between the two members of a pair before the February reslot was 34 metres.

Four of the 41 pairs sit in allergen-segregated stock, so any move of those particular faces needs a food-safety review first.

## Associate feedback card

A feedback card was left out at the Thursday shift meeting. 22 associates completed it. 15 of the 22 selected "the new layout is confusing" from a list of six statements.

Attendance at that meeting was voluntary. The 22 respondents are not a random sample of the 340 picking associates.

## Limitations

Observation covered 24 hours of a 168-hour week, in a single week in July. Associates knew they were being watched.

31 observed errors is too few to break down by shift with any confidence.

No observation was made of any period before the February reslot; the fieldwork post-dates it by five months. Nothing here establishes what the error rate at these faces would be if the pairs were separated again.

The pick-to-light area in aisles 45 to 52 was not observed.
