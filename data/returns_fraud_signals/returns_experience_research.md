# Returns Experience Study

> **Synthetic document.** Fictional research and fictional participants. No real Walmart data.
> Owner: research. Author: Rowan Pike, UX Research. Created: 2026-04-14. Last updated: 2026-04-28.

## Why this study was run

Commissioned by the e-commerce team to inform a checkout redesign, not to study returns
abuse. The brief was to understand why customers return apparel bought online.

## Method and its limits

Twelve interviews, 45 minutes each, conducted remotely in March 2026.

Participants were recruited from customers who volunteered through the loyalty newsletter
and who had made at least one return in the previous six months. This is a self-selected
sample of customers who return things and who are engaged enough to read the newsletter.
It is not representative of the customer base and it is far too small to support any
proportion quoted about it.

## Finding 1 — ordering two sizes is routine

Nine of the twelve said they routinely order two sizes of the same garment intending to
return one. Eight of those nine said they do this because they do not trust the size chart.

> "I order the medium and the large because the medium in one line fits and in another it
> does not." — P-07

> "I would stop ordering two if the sizes were reliable." — P-11

Participants did not describe this as gaming the policy. Two were surprised to be asked
whether it might be a problem for the business.

## Finding 2 — size-chart audit

Alongside the interviews, forty apparel SKUs were measured against the published size
chart. Fourteen differed by more than one size increment, and all fourteen are in the
Tarnside line introduced in 2025-Q3.

The audit did not weight SKUs by sales volume, and no link was made between the affected
SKUs and actual return volumes. The audit says the size chart is wrong for those SKUs. It
does not say how many returns that causes.

## Finding 3 — returns label discovery in the app

Eight of twelve participants could not locate the returns QR code in the mobile app on
first attempt. Three gave up and used the printed slip in the box. This has been passed to
the app team and is tracked separately from returns work.

## What this study cannot speak to

The study did not ask about, and cannot speak to, deliberate misuse of the returns policy.
No participant was asked whether they had ever returned worn or used merchandise, and no
returned item was inspected as part of this work.
